Privacy

Your outdoor data stays yours.

Last updated: October 17, 2026

EcoTrek is an outdoor activity app built for the Congressional App Challenge. This policy covers the website, the Android WebView app, and optional native or backend configurations.

The short version: Your profile, activities, points, tree progress, ratings, and settings are saved on your device by default. EcoTrek requests foreground location only when a feature needs it. Online trail, photo, map, and weather features contact their providers. EcoTrek does not sell data or show ads. There is no user-facing Grok or AI chatbot.

What is saved on your device

Your local profile may include your name, age, height, weight, and optional profile photo. Activity records may include route coordinates, distance, duration, elevation, timestamps, trail completion, and cleanup counts. Points, badges, streaks, ratings, virtual-tree care, and settings are also stored locally. The profile photo is resized and saved on-device; EcoTrek does not upload it. Removing the app or clearing website storage can erase local information.

For faster browsing, EcoTrek may save a public trail catalogue and its last coarse search area on this device. Cache coordinates are rounded to two decimal places and are not a recorded route. Saved trails are identified as saved rather than represented as a current location search.

Location and external services

EcoTrek requests foreground location when you open nearby-trail discovery, request local weather, or start an activity. Location is used to find nearby trails, load local weather, center maps, and record a route while an activity is running. The website and Android WebView app do not request background location. You can deny or revoke access in browser or Android settings. Without a real location, EcoTrek does not substitute Austin or another city.

When you use these features, your device may send the following information directly to service providers:

  • OpenStreetMap Overpass receives the coordinates needed to search for nearby mapped trails.
  • Optional EcoTrek API may receive a request for its public approved-trail catalogue when a deployment configures it. The app does not include GPS coordinates in that catalogue request.
  • Open-Meteo and the U.S. National Weather Service receive coordinates for local forecasts, air quality, or alerts.
  • OpenStreetMap tile service receives map-tile requests that identify the visible viewport, which can be centered on your location, as well as ordinary IP address, browser/referrer, and request metadata. Tiles are not prefetched. If they fail, GPS recording can continue with a local route sketch.
  • Wikimedia Commons may receive a listed trailhead coordinate or trail-name search to find community trail photos.
  • Google Maps receives trailhead information if you choose the “Directions in Google Maps” link. Separately built native Android apps may use Google Maps Platform when configured; the website and downloadable WebView app use OpenStreetMap tiles.

These services apply their own privacy policies and retention practices. Trail and weather lookups do not include your name or activity history.

Optional backend trail information

EcoTrek has no user-facing chatbot, does not ask you to connect a Grok account, and does not send a conversation history to an AI chat provider. A custom deployment may configure an optional server-side trail-intelligence service. If that feature is enabled and invoked, the selected trail’s public catalogue details (such as its name, area, distance, difficulty, and description) may be sent by the server to its configured model provider to prepare trail information. User-entered chat questions, profile details, precise GPS coordinates, and recorded route history are not part of that request. The deployment operator must describe any server-side provider and retention settings it enables.

Sharing a trail

If you choose “Share with friends” from a trail detail, EcoTrek sends a short message containing that selected trail’s name, public details, a map link, and up to two safe photo links to your device’s system share sheet. The app does not upload the message to EcoTrek; you choose whether and where to send it. Recipients and any sharing service you select receive the content under their own terms.

Optional backend storage

A custom deployment may configure an EcoTrek API and database. The current app works without a server or EcoTrek account and uses local guest profiles; guest profile and activity data do not sync to that API. Other API features or custom deployments may send different data to a server, in which case the operator’s storage, access, and deletion terms apply.

What EcoTrek does not do

  • It does not sell or rent personal data or show advertising.
  • It does not share your recorded route with other users.
  • It does not request background location in the website or WebView app.
  • It does not guarantee that a trail, map, weather report, or trail description reflects current conditions. Check official local information before you go.

Your choices

  • Deny or revoke location permission in browser or device settings.
  • Remove your profile photo or clear local app/site data.
  • Choose whether to open external photo, map, and directions links.
  • Contact the team with privacy questions or requests.

Children and security

EcoTrek is not directed at children under 13. Requests use HTTPS where the provider supports it. No system is perfectly secure; if you discover a security issue, contact the team privately.
