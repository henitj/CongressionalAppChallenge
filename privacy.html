Privacy

Your outdoor data stays yours.

Last updated: October 13, 2026

EcoTrek is an outdoor activity tracker made for the Congressional App Challenge. This policy covers the website and the Android WebView APK.

The short version: Your profile, photo, walks, points and settings are saved on your device by default; the current release has no sign-in. Location is used for nearby-trail discovery and while an activity is recording. Online features contact their providers as described below. EcoTrek does not sell data or show ads.

What is saved on your device

Your local profile may include your name, age, height, weight and optional profile photo. Activity records can include route coordinates, distance, duration, elevation, timestamps, trail completion and cleanup counts. Points, badges, streaks, settings and a small app-usage log are also stored locally. The profile photo is resized and stored on-device; EcoTrek does not upload it. Uninstalling the app or clearing website data can remove local information.

Location and online features

EcoTrek requests foreground location when you open Trails or tap Start. It uses location for nearby-trail lookup, local weather, map centering and route recording while an activity is running. The website and Android WebView APK do not request background location. You can deny or revoke access in browser or Android settings.

When you use these features, your device may send request information directly to:

  • OpenStreetMap Overpass and Nominatim receive nearby or reverse-geocoding coordinates to find trail records and a region label.
  • Open-Meteo and the U.S. National Weather Service receive coordinates for local forecasts, air quality or alerts.
  • Google Maps Platform, when its client key is configured, receives map viewport/center and map-rendering requests. The key is visible in the website/APK client and should be referrer-restricted. Without a key, EcoTrek shows a local route sketch instead.
  • Wikimedia Commons receives trailhead coordinates or trail-name searches to find community photos.

These services apply their own privacy policies and retention practices. EcoTrek does not send your name or saved activity history with trail, weather or photo lookups.

Online assistant

On the website and Android WebView APK, EcoTrek uses Grok through Puter by default; no personal AI key is required. Puter receives your question, up to eight recent user/assistant turns, and relevant trail/weather facts. The prompt does not include your precise GPS coordinates, profile details or recorded route history. Do not include sensitive personal information in a prompt. Puter may require its own consent or account flow, and its privacy terms apply.

If Grok cannot be reached, EcoTrek labels the local trail-data answer as an offline fallback. Native builds can optionally use a backend or a personal OpenAI-compatible provider key. A personal key is stored in local app storage and sent over HTTPS to the provider selected by the user; it can be removed in Trail Assistant settings. The provider's privacy terms apply.

Optional backend

The current release does not require a server or account. A custom deployment may enable an EcoTrek API/database for cross-device features. Its operator must describe the information it collects and its retention; do not assume a custom deployment is local-only.

Your choices

  • Choose whether to allow location in browser or Android settings.
  • Remove your profile photo, or erase locally saved data in the app's data settings or through browser storage.
  • Stay offline to avoid online assistant requests; local trail-data fallback remains available. Avoid entering sensitive information in prompts.
  • Restrict Google Maps browser keys to the production site and the Android WebView origin, appassets.ecotrek.app. Treat client keys as public, not secret.

Children and security

EcoTrek is intended for people age 13 and older and is not designed for children under 13. Requests use HTTPS where supported. No system is perfectly secure.
